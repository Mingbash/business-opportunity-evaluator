# Time-specific business research protocol

Read this file before a full source-backed analysis. Use it selectively for compact evaluations when current facts are not already available.

## Scope before search

Record:

- evaluation cutoff date;
- target geography, language, and segment;
- payer, user, trigger, job/outcome, offer, and revenue mechanism;
- the user-specified acquisition path, preserved as the original version;
- company/main-business or side-hustle objective;
- user's relevant channel, assets, constraints, time, and budget when known.

If geography is unspecified, infer and state a scope from the buyer job and delivery model; ask only if alternatives could reverse the decision. Do not default to the user’s residence or to a global market. Distinguish theoretical capacity, model-serviceable capacity and user-reachable buyers.

## Evidence order

Prefer current, primary, and transaction-adjacent sources:

1. official product, pricing, policy, marketplace, regulatory, procurement, filing, or dataset sources;
2. observable transaction/adoption evidence with its exact unit and time period;
3. buyer workflows, job postings, reviews, implementation documentation, and current alternatives;
4. founder/seller self-reports, clearly labeled and cross-checked when consequential;
5. secondary estimates only when their scope and method are usable.

Do not turn vendor claims, store-wide sales, platform users, downloads, or historical cumulative counts into product revenue, active customers, market share, or user acquisition evidence.

An isolated job post, product listing, or review proves an occurrence within its stated scope. It does not by itself establish recurring demand, market capacity, a typical price, or a repeatable acquisition route.

## Required research distinctions

### Demand

Separate:

- recurring buyer problem;
- usage frequency;
- purchase/repurchase or flow of new buyers;
- willingness to pay and budget ownership;
- global potential, business-model-serviceable capacity, and user-reachable buyers.

### Competition and substitutes

For the old solution and each material substitute, investigate:

- why the old solution is insufficient;
- which parts of the buyer's full job it completes;
- acceptance, integration, responsibility, and maintenance gaps;
- target-market penetration/adoption, separately from feature availability;
- price and acquisition route;
- likely supply expansion or contraction over 1–3 years and the mechanism driving it.

Conclude whether demand, substitutable supply, or both are expanding faster, with the mechanism and confidence. Do not stop at “competition is strong”; state current coverage, failure points, remaining paid work, and whether that work is likely to widen or shrink.

Search direct competitors, adjacent products, platform-native features, free tools, generic AI, DIY/self-build, internal teams, manual workflows, and service providers. Competitor presence can prove demand; absence can signal no market.

### Distribution and transaction

Separate:

- where buyers exist;
- whether a seller can reach them;
- whether this user already has an effective channel.

Count dependencies such as marketplace eligibility, search ranking, partner cooperation, audience trust, access to lists, procurement, payment collection, data rights, and implementation authorization. A directory listing or platform audience is not a completed sales path.

For each proposed route, separate five stages: identify, reach, elicit a relevant response, close/collect, and deliver. State which stages are evidenced and which are assumptions.

Before treating an intermediary as a channel or buyer, answer:

- What job, capacity, or access does it lack despite its existing capabilities?
- Why would it outsource or share margin rather than do the work internally?
- What complementary asset does the seller provide: overflow capacity, specialist skill, lower complete cost, geography/language/compliance access, white-label delivery, or another evidenced advantage?
- Is there observable outsourcing, supplier recruitment, overflow work, a product gap, or a buying signal?

If the intermediary's core capability already covers the offer and none of these conditions is supported, classify it as a competitor or self-sufficient provider. Do not keep searching for partners merely to avoid evaluating direct acquisition.

When the user supplies a concrete route—such as marketplace search, Google Maps plus tailored outreach, or direct creator sampling—investigate that route first. If public data can test the hypothesis, run a bounded representative sample before assigning interviews or outreach to the user. Alternative routes may be compared, but never silently replace the specified business.

### Economics and labor

Include discovery, acquisition, trust-building, sales, contracting, payment delay, delivery, customization, revisions, support, maintenance, refunds, tool/data costs, platform fees, and channel share. Separate one-time cash, net cash contribution, and ARR/recurring revenue.

## Market capacity

Use the narrowest defensible scope. Provide a concise range and one or two lines of method by default. A full bottom-up model is warranted only if it could change the decision or the user requests it.

Never infer a paid market from a broad industry total or multiply an arbitrary penetration rate without labeling it a scenario. Do not present a scenario range as a statistical confidence interval.

## Analysis and uncertainty

Tag consequential statements as:

- **Fact:** directly supported and within source scope;
- **Inference:** reasoned from facts, with the chain stated;
- **Scenario:** assumption used to understand economics or capacity;
- **Unknown:** not established; never treated as zero or absent.

When evidence changes an old conclusion, provide the replacement judgment and explain how it changes the ranking, entry, or next action.

## Stop condition

Before broad collection, identify the 1–2 uncertainties most likely to reverse the tier, ranking, or entry route and research those first. Do not announce a preferred opportunity while its payer need, payable gap, or only acquisition path remains an untested assumption.

Do not research indefinitely. Stop when the remaining uncertainty would not change the tier, chosen entry, or next bounded test. Surface only the 1–3 unresolved questions most likely to change the decision. Complete all feasible desktop research before assigning user interviews, private data, authorization, or relationship work to the user.
