# Website templates as a side business

[简体中文](website-paths.zh-CN.md) · [Back to the README](../README.md)

This case reconstructs a September 26, 2026 assessment of three website template businesses. The evidence was scoped to the marketplaces and buyers sampled at the time. The proposed tests are plans from that assessment, with outcomes still unknown here.

## Decision overview

The goal was side income with limited build and maintenance time. Each row has a different payer or deliverable. Changing the marketplace for the same template would be a different route within that row.

| Path | Buyer and purchase | What gets delivered | How to charge and reach buyers | Historical decision and first test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General website template | Individuals and small businesses buy a starting layout | A general-purpose site template | One-time personal license. Etsy was a source for comparison. Seller eligibility and any actual sales channel needed checking. | **Pass.** Free and inexpensive alternatives already met much of the need. Revisit if there is evidence of a specific workflow they leave unfinished. |
| Framer kit for one agency use case | Small agencies and freelance designers buy a way to deliver the same kind of project faster | A site, reusable components, content examples, form instructions, and handoff materials. Buyers cover hosting and domains. | One-site pack or a team license. Reach agencies that build similar sites repeatedly and check the Framer creator route separately. | **Test first, on a small scale.** Pick one kind of project, show an agency the handoff and reuse flow, and test whether it will pay for a first license. If buyers only want bespoke work, revisit the case for reusable sales. |
| Squarespace booking site for bridal makeup artists | Independent artists and small studios buy a way to turn portfolio visits into inquiries and bookings | A portfolio template plus an example inquiry and booking flow | One-time license. Show the preview in a relevant practitioner community or on an independent demo page. | **Conditional test.** Show a preview to ten artists and look for two preorders before building the full template. |

## Opportunity detail

| What to check | What the research suggested and what remained open |
|---|---|
| Paid demand and market capacity | Marketplace listings showed offers existed. They left actual sales, repeat demand and reachable buyer counts uncertain. |
| Competition and substitutes | General templates had free and low-cost substitutes. The two narrower offers depended on an unfinished job: agency handoff and reuse, or the artist’s inquiry and booking flow. |
| Distribution | Test agencies through a specific project type and test artists with a preview. A marketplace submission has its own eligibility, listing and discovery conditions. |
| Entry and first cash | An original sample, a way to collect payment and access to relevant buyers all mattered. Time to a first paid order remained unknown. |
| Unit economics | Count setup, revisions, support, fees and refunds. Reusable components help only if new buyers can use them with limited custom work. |
| Solution | General layout, agency delivery kit and bridal booking template are three different offers. The last two include work beyond page styling. |
| Business model | A site license brings in cash once. A team license could support recurring revenue if buyers keep using the kit and updates are worth maintaining. |
| Timing and durability | The research did not establish a durable window. Future changes to site builders, competing templates or booking integrations could change the reason to buy. |

## Scoring and gates

The workbook used a six-part side-business rubric. The eight rows above map the findings to today’s output fields. The original scores stay on the original scale. The current Market Structure, Current Entry and Overall tiers remain unassigned in this historical example. The main gates were a concrete reason to pay, a reachable buyer, and a workable payment and delivery route.

## Assumptions and scenarios

The workbook modeled US$29 for the general template and US$99 for the bridal template as possible test prices. At an assumed 80% cash contribution after direct costs, about 44 or 13 sales a month, respectively, would be needed to contribute US$1,000 before tax and before valuing the founder’s time. Sales volume, fee rates and time per buyer were still open. For the agency kit, the useful next observation is a paid order for a reusable license. One buyer’s request for a custom build would support a different offer.
